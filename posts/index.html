1986 was a good year

2026-09-21

I was 13 in 1986. That year was epic for me, for several reasons. I had just passed the exam to jump from junior high school to senior high school, Summer was being sweet to me , spending the holidays at my Aunt’s place. Uncle Nino and Auntie Borina were absolutely the best, they would let me play outside and do all sorts of mischief (I even risked incinerating an entire field near the house one day) and in the evening they would always have friends and neighbors coming and spending time on that beautiful terrace in their villa near Riesi. One day Uncle and Dad came back from a trip and gave me one hell of a gift for my promotion: a Commodore 128. That was, for me, the beginning of everything. Thanks to a friend of my Aunt’s: Professor Lo Giudice, I learned BASIC and a bit of assembly there, started programming , started loving machines and that love survived until now. Commodore 128 was a bit of a crazy mash-up of everything, had a 128 mode with a very powerful BASIC, full compatibility with C64 and its games (in C64 mode) and a third more serious mode called CP/M which really puzzled me and that I had no idea how to try (I only had a tape and no disk drives). Long story short, 40 years have passed and now I have decided to give C128 a little extra love, for the sake of memories, for the sake of the old days. So 1986 was born, an emulator which broadly borrows from VICE (Thank you guys, I don’t think it would have been possible to create a Commodore 128 emulator from scratch without your guidance), but unlinke VICE it does not emulate the built-in C64 mode. As far as I can I want 1986 to have nothing to do with C64. C128 was a neglected machine because of its more popular sibiling. I want the C128 to be itself 100% and only run software that it is meant for it. Even if there isn’t and I have to write it. Try it if you want, it’s still buggy as hell and remember I did not write this for you. I wrote it for me. And if I close my eyes a bit I can still see those beautiful Summer sunsets and be a kid once more again.

Browsing the Internet on SymbOS

2026-08-20
#SymbOS  #SymZilla  #MSX  #Amstrad CPC  #Retrocomputing  #Web Browser 

Browsing the modern Internet from an 8-bit computer is no small feat. SymZilla was originally developed by SymbOS’s Prodatron as a document viewer. My contribution was to add networking and support for displaying simplified HTML pages, helping bring web browsing to SymbOS on classic machines.

The browser relies on GB-proxy, a companion proxy designed to reduce the complexity of today’s web. GB-proxy retrieves pages on behalf of the client, handles SSL/TLS connections, strips pages down to a simpler form of HTML, and shrinks and converts images into a format SymbOS can display. This lets the browser concentrate on presenting a practical, lightweight version of the web within the limits of the platform.

PS/2 to MSX mouse with an Arduino Nano

2026-08-19
#MSX  #Arduino  #hardware  #mouse 

I was able to put together a cheap PS/2-to-MSX mouse interface using an Arduino Nano. The project works by using the Arduino Nano as the bridge between a standard PS/2 mouse and the MSX mouse port.

The design is based on denjhang’s MSX Nano Mouse 2025 repository, which provides both the Gerber files for the PCB and the firmware source code. Having those files available made the build straightforward and inexpensive: I could order the board, assemble the small set of components, flash the Nano, and connect a PS/2 mouse.

Smoke testing the Omega MSX

2026-08-19
#MSX  #Omega MSX  #repair  #retrocomputing  #hardware 

After some initial setbacks, we are finally making progress with the Omega MSX.

The first problem was a defective Yamaha 8139 chip, which prevented the system from booting at all. Once that was resolved, we were able to get the machine to display C-BIOS and its diagnostic screens.

That revealed a second fault: capacitor C64 was measuring only 68 µF, rather than its specified 220 µF. This was responsible for a strange yellow ghosting effect on the display.
